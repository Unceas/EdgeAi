0. The One-Minute Mental Model

Think of the smart prosthetic hand as two brains working together:

  • Brain that Listens (ML Classifier): Simulated 2-channel sEMG → Time-domain features → Scikit-Learn Random Forest → Predicts user intention (Open Hand, Power Grip, Pinch Grip, Release) in 1.5 ms.
  • Brain that Feels (Adaptive Controller): Simulated FSR tactile pressure → Closed-loop feedback controller → Dynamically steps servo effort, maintains stable grip within deadband, and injects an immediate +18% torque boost if slip is detected.
  • Scope: The current build proves this complete pipeline in software. Physical electrodes, tactile sensors, servos, 3D hand mechanics, and embedded ESP32 deployment represent the scheduled Phase-2 hardware integration.

6. Jargon You Must Know (Plain English)

Term Simple, Defensible Explanation
sEMG Surface Electromyography: tiny electrical voltage pulses generated when muscle fibers contract, measured on forearm skin.
Edge AI Running a machine learning model directly on an on-device microcontroller (like an ESP32) without needing internet, cloud, or GPUs.
Random Forest An ensemble machine learning classifier composed of small decision trees voting together. Fast, deterministic, and ideal for microcontrollers.
Feature Extraction Converting raw waveform time series into concise statistical numbers (RMS, MAV, Variance, Energy, ZCR) that the AI understands.
RMS / MAV / ZCR Root Mean Square (power level), Mean Absolute Value (contraction magnitude), and Zero Crossing Rate (frequency content).
FSR Sensor Force-Sensitive Resistor: a sensor pad whose electrical resistance drops as mechanical pressure increases, measuring contact in Newtons.
Closed-Loop Control A control system that measures actual sensor output, compares it against the desired setpoint, and continuously corrects motor commands.
Adaptive Grip Automatically adjusting finger squeeze force based on real-time touch feedback rather than blindly driving motors to a fixed angle.
Calibration Acquiring a 2-second resting baseline reading of an individual user to adjust noise floor offsets and muscle gain multipliers.
Deadband A ±3.0 Newton tolerance window around target pressure preventing motor jitter and servo overheating from tiny sensor fluctuations.

8. Faculty Q&A — Short, Safe, Bulletproof Answers

Q1: Why don't you have physical hardware connected?
“This showcase is the Phase-1 software validation milestone. In bionic robotics, debugging mechanical failures, noisy bio-electrodes, and ML logic simultaneously causes high failure rates. Validating signal processing, AI inference, and closed-loop slip reflexes digitally ensures our control architecture is verified before hardware fabrication.”
Q2: Is the EMG signal real?
“No, the sEMG signal in this phase is programmatically simulated using frequency-shaped Gaussian process noise based on physiological muscle recruitment literature. Real MyoWare surface electrodes are scheduled for Phase 2.”
Q3: Is the AI running on an ESP32 right now?
“Not in this current software demo. The model is running locally on the computer host. However, our Random Forest uses only 35 shallow trees and takes under 30 KB of memory, which directly fits into an ESP32-S3's 512 KB SRAM.”
Q4: Why Random Forest instead of Deep Learning (CNN/LSTM)?
“Deep learning requires heavy memory, high battery draw, and complex GPU/NPU acceleration incompatible with low-cost prosthetics. Random Forest gives near-instant classification (1.5 ms) on time-domain features with minimal battery consumption.”
Q5: Why is the accuracy 100%? Isn't that unrealistic?
“Yes, absolutely. That is why it is explicitly labeled on the dashboard as ‘SYNTHETIC BENCHMARK — NOT CLINICAL VALIDATION’. Because synthetic signals have clean mathematical separation, the model easily achieves 100%. On real human subjects with skin impedance, sweat, and muscle fatigue, we anticipate 85% to 92% accuracy.”
Q6: What is the difference between open-loop and closed-loop control here?
“Open-loop blindly drives servos to a preset angle regardless of payload size or fragility. Closed-loop continuously measures FSR pressure, compares it to target thresholds, and adaptively modulates servo duty cycle to maintain grip and prevent slippage.”
Q7: How does the anti-slip reflex work?
“When tactile shear vibration or a slip event is detected, the controller bypasses steady-state regulation and executes a rapid compensatory force boost of +18% to re-establish friction hold and arrest payload drop.”
Q8: What prevents the prosthetic hand from crushing a fragile object?
“The controller enforces an anti-crush safety ceiling calibrated at 75 Newtons. If sensor pressure exceeds this ceiling, the controller instantly reduces motor torque.”
Q9: What sampling rate and window size are you using?
“A 1000 Hz sampling rate with a 200 ms sliding observation window (200 samples per channel). 200 ms is the established clinical standard because any latency under 300 ms is perceived as instantaneous by the human nervous system.”
Q10: Does the machine learning model continuously learn online?
“Not in the current version. The system performs user calibration (baseline noise floor and MVC scaling) and real-time closed-loop force adaptation. Continual online model retraining is reserved for future exploration.”
Q11: Is this considered a medical device?
“No. This is an engineering clinic prototype designed for academic proof-of-concept validation, not a certified clinical medical device.”
Q12: What is the main innovation if you didn't invent a new ML algorithm?
“The primary innovation is the architectural integration: combining lightweight edge-compatible intention classification with closed-loop tactile force regulation and anti-slip reflexes in a single unified prototype.”
